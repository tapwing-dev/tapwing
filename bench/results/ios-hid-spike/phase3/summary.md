## 3. The two smoke anomalies

### Why the alert `tapOn` is not faster in `--input native`

Measured. In `--input native` the `touch` call drops from about 335 ms to about 12 to 15 ms, and the step is still 878 ms (`f5_cs`) or 1012 ms (`f5_cs_lazy`) against 900 and 915 ms with the flag off. The step is bounded by two animations, not by call cost.

The frame events come from the settle loop of `native_core/ops.rs` (`perform_tap`, which calls `surface.settle` before and after the tap) running on the runner-backed surface in `ios_native_lane/ops.rs` (`settle`, loop at line 134). With the flag on, `IosNativeClient::is_screen_static` answers from `HidSession::screen_static`, which takes two framebuffer frames of about 25 to 30 ms each. A poll is two frames plus the 50 ms `STATIC_POLL` sleep, so about 110 ms. Each pair of `frame` events in the log is one poll. The runner path does the same loop with one `isScreenStatic` HTTP call per poll, 120 to 160 ms each, and the call has no extra sleep.

The two animations are the alert appearing and the alert closing.

- Before the touch, the runner path needs one poll (180 ms) and the HID path needs four polls (456 ms, 8 frame events). With the flag on the earlier steps finish sooner, so the alert is still animating when the step starts. The touch can only go out once two frames match. The time from the Request touch to the alert touch is 578 ms with HID and 1087 ms with the runner (table below), so HID taps within roughly one poll (110 ms) of the earliest possible moment. There is nothing left to save before the touch.
- After the touch, the runner needs the 335 ms touch call (which blocks while the alert closes) plus two polls, 717 ms in total. HID needs 425 ms (`f5_cs`) or 569 ms (`f5_cs_lazy`), 4 to 5 polls. The closing animation and the Flutter rebuild bound this.

The saving shows up in the neighbouring steps, because those steps lose the runner call overhead. `f5_cs_lazy` native, medians off to on: `tapOn playground_button` 1068 to 241 ms, `tapOn "Request Location Permission"` 1062 to 632 ms, alert `tapOn` 915 to 1012 ms.

Net gain of the client routing (point (a)), measured and per flow (5 passed runs per cell, 6 for `f5_cs_lazy` native):

- `f5_cs` auto: 9889 to 9580 ms, 310 ms or 3.1 percent. The alert `tapOn` gains 200 ms (932 to 733). The alert `assertVisible` gains 37 ms. `assertVisible "Permission: granted.*"` loses 23 ms (see below). All other steps are unchanged because in `auto` Flutter taps do not use the runner.
- `f5_cs` native: 8234 to 6954 ms, 1279 ms or 15.5 percent. Gains come from Flutter element taps (830 ms and 411 ms), `setOrientation` (83 ms). The alert `tapOn` gains 22 ms. `assertVisible "Permission: granted.*"` loses 76 ms.
- `f5_cs_lazy` native: 6441 to 5165 ms, 1276 ms or 19.8 percent. The alert `tapOn` loses 97 ms.
- Per step: a Flutter tap gains 400 to 830 ms, the alert tap gains nothing in native mode, a `setOrientation` gains at most 140 ms.

Inferred. `--input native` is not the default, so the routing only helps users who ask for it or flows that already warm the runner (any flow with `setOrientation`). In `auto` it does little, and it adds one AX read per focus probe (below).

### Why `assertVisible "Permission: granted.*"` took 275 ms in the smoke run

Measured. The smoke log has one `hid route=snapshot` event of 246 ms right before that step. `ios_focus_probe` in `run/hybrid.rs` (line 544) asks `hid_focus()` first, on every focus probe, even when the runner is already running. That is one accessibility snapshot of depth 1 added to the step. In the 6 flag-on `f5_cs` auto runs of this benchmark (warm-up plus 5) the snapshot took 23 to 27 ms in that step and the step took 50 to 53 ms against 27 ms off. Over all flag-on benchmark runs (excluding warm-ups) the successful snapshot events range from 3 to 42 ms. The 246 ms read was a one-off and is not reproducible: 0 of 6 runs at that position, 0 of 45 successful snapshot events in the flag-on benchmark runs above 42 ms.

So the steady cost is about 25 ms per focus probe while the runner is up. It buys nothing there, because the runner already answers the same question in 1 ms (`runningApp`). In `f5_cs` native the same step is 22 ms off and 98 ms on, from an extra `viewHierarchy` call in one run, which is run noise (min 21 ms, one outlier of 101 ms).

Method (measured). `tapcheck.m` in this directory links the shim and uses one process and one HID client for all taps, as Tapwing does. It taps the Favorite switch of Product 1 with a 10 ms hold (`TAP_HOLD_MS`) and reads the switch colour from the framebuffer after 700 ms. A tap whose state did not change is read again after 1.5 s to tell a late delivery from a lost one. The app had been running for tens of seconds before the first tap. The accessibility tree could not be used for the read, because with the simulator accessibility flags at 0 the app tree is empty (this matches phase 1). Navigation to the detail screen uses `flows/nav_detail.yaml` with Tapwing and the flag off.

Result (measured). 120 of 120 on the main simulator, 240 of 240 over 6 boots of the temporary simulator (40 per boot), 20 of 20 Tapwing runs with exactly one delivered HID touch each. No tap was lost and none was late. This does not reproduce the "dropped on some boots and intermittently within one" that the research quotes from idb for CoreSimulator 1155.4 and later. It does not disprove it either. The test covers one device type, one runtime, one app, a switch tap with a 10 ms hold, 360 taps, and a booted simulator that was idle. I did not test swipes, buttons, long presses, a busy host, or a first boot of a fresh runtime. Boots 2 to 6 were ready in 2 to 7 s, which means they were warm boots of the same device. Only boot 1 was a first boot (21 s).

Note on Tapwing's own retry. `perform_tap` repeats a tap once when the screen signature did not change (`retryTapIfNoChange`, on by default). A lost HID tap is therefore re-sent once on every `tapOn`, with the flag on or off. It does not cover `longPressOn`, swipes, Home, Lock, or taps with the retry turned off. In all 20 runs the log shows a single touch, so no retry fired.

Failures of the 20 runs: none. Several `hid route=snapshot ok=0 "no frontmost application"` events appear in every run. They are expected while an app is in front and the accessibility flags are 0. They cost 3 to 6 ms (30 ms on the first), and the router treats them as "not SpringBoard".

## 6. Code review

Method. I read the diff `a265b6c..da46ab7` for `crates/` in full, ran the forced-failure paths, and used the logs above. Line numbers are in the files at HEAD. "Confirmed" means seen in code or in a log. "Suspicion" means a plausible failure that I did not trigger.

### Confirmed defects

1. **Every focus probe pays for an accessibility read, even with the runner up.** `run/hybrid.rs:548` calls `hid_focus()` at the start of `ios_focus_probe`, before the check of the runner slot. Measured: about 25 ms per probe, `assertVisible "Permission: granted.*"` 27 to 50 ms in `f5_cs` auto, 5 of 5 runs. The runner answers in 1 ms. Fix direction is to ask HID only while the slot is not started. Sure.
2. **A miss on the HID surface is final and never reaches the runner.** `hid/surface.rs:265` (`is_final`) treats `MaestroError` and `AssertionError` as final, and `run/hybrid.rs:796` returns them. With the flag on, an element that the XCUITest tree has and the AXPTranslator tree lacks fails the step after the whole remaining budget, where flag off would pass. Phase 1 showed the trees differ: no `Alert` node, empty `AXTitle`, app trees empty at accessibility flags 0. The alert in `f5_cs_lazy` works, but any other SpringBoard screen (a system sheet, Control Center, a share sheet) can differ. I confirmed the code path, not a failing flow. Fairly sure it matters.
3. **A failed multi-point touch leaves the finger down.** `shim.m:364` sends a down message at every point and the up message only after the loop. If `hid_send` fails at point k (5 s acknowledge timeout or a rejected message), `ap_touch` returns with `[sent]` and no up message. The host reports an error and does not repeat, which is right, but the simulator keeps a pressed touch, which may turn the next tap into a drag or be ignored. Sure about the code, unsure about the effect.
4. **State shared across simulators.** `shim.m:744` keeps one `static bridge`, `shim.m:650` sets the delegate of the `AXPTranslator` singleton on every snapshot, and `device_cache()` (`shim.m:166`) is an `NSMutableDictionary` without a lock. `HidSession` serializes per simulator only. With `--shard` over two simulators and the flag on, two snapshots can run at once. One job can then answer its accessibility requests through the other device's bridge, or two threads can mutate the dictionary together. Sure about the shared state, not tested.
5. **Timeouts add up to long hangs.** `ap_snapshot` waits `6 x timeout` (`shim.m:770`). The surface reads with `SNAPSHOT_TIMEOUT_S = 3.0` (`surface.rs:35`), so one hierarchy read can block for 18 s, and `poll_find` can start several. The focus question uses 1.0 s, so up to 6 s per probe. After the wait expires, the worker keeps running on the singleton translator while the next call replaces its delegate. A step with a 2 s budget can overrun by an order of magnitude, and the read has no failure path that returns early. Sure about the bounds, not triggered.
6. **A lost HID input is reported as success.** The shim only learns that SimulatorKit accepted the message (`hid_send` completion). It cannot see delivery. This is inherent to the legacy path. Measured loss was 0 of 380 taps, so the cost is zero on this machine, but the code has no verification and relies on `retryTapIfNoChange` for `tapOn` only. Swipes, Home, Lock and long presses return success with no check.

### Suspicions

7. **Early diversion at app launch.** For a lazy runner slot the old code waited 2000 ms (`IOS_LAZY_PROBE_MS`) on Flutter before it asked who was in front. With the flag on it asks after 400 ms (`hybrid.rs:992` and `:1025`). While SpringBoard owns the screen (launch animation, relaunch after a crash, `stopApp`, home screen) a Flutter lookup that has not found its element yet is diverted to the SpringBoard tree with the full remaining budget and ends as a final miss (finding 2), where the old path would have found the element after 1.6 s more. The 25 runs in this study did not hit it, because `launchApp` returns after the driver connects.
8. **The alert path fires on any portrait SpringBoard.** `tree.rs` `front_of` returns `SpringBoard` for the home screen, lock screen, Control Center, app switcher and notification shade. The runner path also runs natively there, so the intent is the same, but the data come from a different tree (see 2), and Tapwing taps with a legacy touch where the runner used XCUITest. I did not exercise those screens. Typing on those screens falls back to the runner (`unsupported`), which is correct.
9. **Orientation.** `client.rs` `portrait` only changes on `setOrientation` through the client. A rotation by the app, by `simctl`, or a rotated interface that was already there at start is detected only when `device_info` shows width greater than height, and `device_info` is cached. Upside-down portrait looks like portrait (width less than height), so touches would be mirrored. The frame is always the unrotated one. Not tested.
10. **Hit-test fallback (12 points, `shim.m:671`).** Cost measured with an app in front at flags 0: every one of the 12 requests misses and the whole failed snapshot takes 3 to 6 ms (30 ms the first time). Cheap. A hit test is an accessibility query and I know of no input side effect, but a query to a Flutter app can switch on its semantics tree (an AX client was not there before). I did not measure that. A second risk is wrong attribution: the points start at 0.3 of the height, and when the alert does not cover that point, the answer is the app behind it, so a real alert could be reported as "app in front". It only matters when `frontmostApplication` itself returns nil.
11. **`x86_64-apple-darwin`.** `build.rs` gates on `target_os == macos` only, so the shim is compiled and shipped for Intel. The research (Q5) says an Intel Mac stops at Xcode 26.6 (before the Indigo change), nobody measured the shim there, and recommends compiling it out. The flag is opt-in and the load fails gracefully when frameworks are missing, but with Xcode 26 the Indigo calls would run unmeasured against a simulator. I could not build this target here (not installed).
12. **Small items.** `ap_frame` does not check `CGImageDestinationCreateWithData` (`shim.m:852`) or `malloc` (`:862`) for NULL. `ap_keys` is compiled in and unused. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` sits in `native/ios_hid/` and, per the research, `scripts/dist.sh` does not ship it. The cached device in `device_cache()` is never dropped if the simulator is rebooted inside one process, and the state check only catches "not booted". No Objective-C exception can reach Rust from the entry points I read, since every `ap_*` function has an `@try` and the snapshot worker has its own. FFI string handling is correct: every shim string is freed once with `ap_free`, and `to_string_lossy` covers non-UTF-8.

## Recommendation

Recommendation: keep it opt-in. Do not make it the default.

Reasons.

- The one large win is real and measured. `f5_cs_lazy` auto goes from 11079 ms to 6147 ms (5/5 passed on both sides, 0 of 5 runs spawned `xcodebuild`), and 20 of 20 further runs passed with exactly one delivered touch each. It is a runner-less alert path, and it is what the issue asked for.
- Outside that case the gain is small or zero. `f5_cs` auto gains 310 ms (3 percent). In `--input native` the Flutter taps gain 400 to 830 ms each, but the alert tap does not, and that mode is rare. The flag adds about 25 ms per focus probe when the runner is up (finding 1).
- The delivery risk from the research did not show up in 380 taps, but my test covered one device, one runtime, one gesture and warm boots, and the shim cannot detect a lost input. The default needs more than that.
- The review has real defects that make a default unsafe. Finding 2 (a miss on the HID tree is final, so a flow that passes today can fail), finding 4 (shared accessibility state when shards run at once), and finding 5 (waits of 6 to 18 s) each need a fix and a test before the flag becomes the default.
- It also relies on private Apple API that idb says is on the way out for buttons and keyboard (Q4), and the shim now compiles on Intel where nobody tested it.

Before widening it, I would fix findings 1, 2 and 5, gate the shim out of `x86_64-apple-darwin`, ship `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` in the tarball, and repeat the reliability test on a busy host and with swipes.

Not verified. Home screen, Control Center, lock screen and app switcher were not exercised. Rotation and landscape were not exercised beyond `f5_cs`. Intel and non-macOS builds were not compiled. The flag-on path under `--shard` was not run. Xcode 26 behaviour is unknown.
