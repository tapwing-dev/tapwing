Method (measured). `tapcheck.m` in this directory links the shim and uses one process and one HID client for all taps, as Tapwing does. It taps the Favorite switch of Product 1 with a 10 ms hold (`TAP_HOLD_MS`) and reads the switch colour from the framebuffer after 700 ms. A tap whose state did not change is read again after 1.5 s to tell a late delivery from a lost one. The app had been running for tens of seconds before the first tap. The accessibility tree could not be used for the read, because with the simulator accessibility flags at 0 the app tree is empty (this matches phase 1). Navigation to the detail screen uses `flows/nav_detail.yaml` with Tapwing and the flag off.

Result (measured). 120 of 120 on the main simulator, 240 of 240 over 6 boots of the temporary simulator (40 per boot), 20 of 20 Tapwing runs with exactly one delivered HID touch each. No tap was lost and none was late. This does not reproduce the "dropped on some boots and intermittently within one" that the research quotes from idb for CoreSimulator 1155.4 and later. It does not disprove it either. The test covers one device type, one runtime, one app, a switch tap with a 10 ms hold, 360 taps, and a booted simulator that was idle. I did not test swipes, buttons, long presses, a busy host, or a first boot of a fresh runtime. Boots 2 to 6 were ready in 2 to 7 s, which means they were warm boots of the same device. Only boot 1 was a first boot (21 s).

Note on Tapwing's own retry. `perform_tap` repeats a tap once when the screen signature did not change (`retryTapIfNoChange`, on by default). A lost HID tap is therefore re-sent once on every `tapOn`, with the flag on or off. It does not cover `longPressOn`, swipes, Home, Lock, or taps with the retry turned off. In all 20 runs the log shows a single touch, so no retry fired.

Failures of the 20 runs: none. Several `hid route=snapshot ok=0 "no frontmost application"` events appear in every run. They are expected while an app is in front and the accessibility flags are 0. They cost 3 to 6 ms (30 ms on the first), and the router treats them as "not SpringBoard".
